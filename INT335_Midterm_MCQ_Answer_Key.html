Thinking Modes

Q1 Case-based

Case: Meera, a third-year B.Tech student, spends three days on a circuit-design problem for her mini-project, trying every method she knows without success. A friend advises her to go for a long walk and stop thinking about it. The next morning, while brushing her teeth, a workable approach suddenly occurs to her.

Which explanation best fits what happened?

  1. A. Her diffuse mode reorganised material she had already struggled with in focused mode and produced a connection. ✔
  2. B. The walk switched her learning style from Visual to Kinesthetic, which solved the problem.
  3. C. Her focused mode kept running unchanged during the walk and simply finished the calculation.
  4. D. Diffuse mode works best on an empty mind, so her three days of effort were unnecessary.

Answer: A

The diffuse mode does not work on an empty mind; it reorganises material you have already struggled with. Rule: struggle first (focused), then step away (diffuse).

Q2 Case-based

Case: Two students have ten days to solve an open design challenge. Arjun reads the brief once on Day 1 and then does nothing for nine days, saying his 'subconscious will work on it'. Neel works intensively for two days, then alternates between short breaks and further work.

Who is more likely to reach a creative breakthrough, and why?

  1. A. Neel, because diffuse processing needs material previously wrestled with in focused mode, and effective problem-solving alternates the two modes. ✔
  2. B. Arjun, because a longer rest gives the diffuse mode more time to build broad connections.
  3. C. Both equally, because switching between modes is random and unrelated to study habits.
  4. D. Neither, because creative breakthroughs depend only on learning style and not on thinking modes.

Answer: A

Arjun gave the diffuse mode nothing to reorganise. Neel struggled first and then stepped away, which is exactly the alternation the notes recommend.

Q3 Case-based

Case: A team lead at a software firm must (i) trace a known bug line by line through an established debugging procedure, and (ii) later come up with an unconventional concept for a new feature.

Which thinking modes best suit tasks (i) and (ii)?

  1. A. (i) Focused mode; (ii) Diffuse mode ✔
  2. B. Both focused, because engineering work is logical
  3. C. Both diffuse, because both tasks involve problem-solving
  4. D. (i) Diffuse mode; (ii) Focused mode

Answer: A

Focused mode = concentrated attention using established pathways (procedures, code, analysis). Diffuse mode = relaxed, associative thinking that makes broader connections (brainstorming, innovative design).

Q4 Theory-based

Diffuse mode = relaxed, associative thinking that makes broader connections. Focused mode handles equations, code, data and procedures.

Q6 Theory-based

Which option correctly contrasts the focused and diffuse thinking modes?

  1. A. Focused: used for brainstorming; Diffuse: used for solving equations
  2. B. Focused: concentrated, analytical, uses established pathways; Diffuse: relaxed, associative, makes broader connections ✔
  3. C. Focused: relaxed and associative; Diffuse: concentrated and procedural

Focused = logical, structured, sequential (equations, code, procedures). Diffuse = creative, exploratory, flexible (brainstorming, innovative design). Effective problem-solving alternates between the two.

Learning Styles (VARK)

Q8 Case-based

Case: A college announces that after a VARK questionnaire every student will be taught only in their preferred style, claiming this 'guarantees higher marks'. A Design Thinking student is asked to comment.

Which critique is most consistent with the course notes?

  1. A. The claim is well supported by controlled studies, so the policy is correct.
  2. B. VARK is a useful prompt for self-reflection, but the claim that teaching in the preferred style improves results has repeatedly failed in controlled studies. ✔
  3. C. VARK is useless even for reflection, so students should avoid thinking about how they study.
  4. D. The research is irrelevant because any idea that becomes popular must be evidence-backed.

Answer: B

Treat a VARK result as a nudge toward self-awareness, not a rule. The notes also stress that an idea can be hugely popular without research support, and that retrieval practice, spacing and interleaving are the strategies that do replicate.

Q9 Case-based

Case: Using Activity 1.2 scoring (V = statements 1+5, A = 2+6, R = 3+7, K = 4+8), a student rates statements 1 to 8 as 2, 4, 5, 1, 3, 2, 4, 3 respectively.

  1. A. V (Visual)

V = 2+3 = 5; A = 4+2 = 6; R = 5+4 = 9; K = 1+3 = 4. R is highest. (Remember this is a reflection prompt, not a rule for how you must study.)

Functional Fixedness & Cognitive Blocks

Q12 Case-based

Case: During a hackathon, a team needs a lightweight stand to hold a sensor above a table. Someone points at an empty cardboard tube from a kitchen roll and says, 'That's just rubbish.' Another member says, 'Hold on — it is hollow, light, rigid and cylindrical,' and uses it as the stand.

Which technique did the second member use to overcome functional fixedness?

  1. A. Rejecting ideas early so the team could converge faster
  2. B. Surveying users on how much they like cardboard tubes
  3. C. Listing the object's properties and finding a use for each, rather than thinking of its usual purpose ✔
  4. D. Following the team's conventional thinking in order to fit in

Answer: C

Functional fixedness is seeing objects only for their conventional purpose. The way out is to stop thinking about the object and start listing its properties, then find a use for each property.

Q13 Case-based

Case: A senior engineer with twenty years' experience is given a new thermal problem. Without studying how it differs from earlier ones, he proposes the same heat-sink-based solution he used in his last six projects.

Which cognitive block is most clearly at work?

  1. A. Premature judgment
  2. B. The expertise trap ✔
  3. C. Fear of failure
  4. D. Conformity

Answer: B

The expertise trap is over-reliance on past solutions: the more you know, the more you reach for what worked before.

Q14 Case-based

  1. A. Fear of failure (criticism); conformity ✔
  2. B. Expertise trap; premature judgment
  3. C. Conformity; fear of failure
  4. D. Premature judgment; expertise trap

Answer: A

Sana hesitates because she fears criticism (fear of failure). The others follow the conventional, already-spoken line of thought to fit in (conformity).

Q15 Theory-based

Creativity, Invention & Innovation

Q21 Case-based

Case: A university lab builds the first working prototype of a battery chemistry that has never existed before, but no company has adopted it. Separately, a start-up takes ordinary existing batteries and builds a battery-swap service for e-rickshaw drivers that thousands use daily.

How should the two be classified according to the course?

  1. A. Lab: creativity only; start-up: invention
  2. B. Both are inventions, since both involve batteries
  3. C. Lab: invention; start-up: innovation ✔
  4. D. Lab: innovation; start-up: invention

Answer: C

Invention = creating something entirely new. Innovation = successfully implementing ideas to create value. Creativity becomes innovation only when it delivers value.

Q22 Case-based

Case: A local tiffin service adds a mobile app for ordering its existing meals. A new firm, with no restaurants, launches shared cloud kitchens that deliver office meals at half the price to people who previously could not afford regular ordering.

Which classification is most accurate?

  1. A. The app is a disruptive innovation; the cloud-kitchen firm is an incremental improvement
  2. B. The app is an incremental improvement; the cloud-kitchen firm is a disruptive innovation ✔
  3. C. Both are disruptive because both use technology
  4. D. Both are incremental because both sell meals

Answer: B

Incremental = small but meaningful improvements to an existing product or process. Disruptive = a new product, service or business model that transforms markets with simpler, cheaper or more accessible solutions and serves unmet needs.

Kinds of Problems

Q28 Case-based

Case: Consider three tasks: (a) compute the maximum bending stress in a beam under given loads; (b) design a mobile app that improves student well-being on a campus; (c) reduce air pollution across a metropolitan region where industry, commuters, farmers and regulators have conflicting interests.

How should the problems be classified?

  1. A. (a) well-defined; (b) ill-defined; (c) wicked ✔
  2. B. (a) ill-defined; (b) wicked; (c) well-defined
  3. C. (a) wicked; (b) ill-defined; (c) well-defined
  4. D. (a) well-defined; (b) wicked; (c) ill-defined

Answer: A

Well-defined: clear goals and one correct answer. Ill-defined: unclear goals or paths, many solutions (app design). Wicked: complex and interconnected, answers are better or worse, not right or wrong (climate, poverty, healthcare).

Q29 Theory-based

Which is a feature of wicked problems?

  1. A. They are complex and interconnected, and solutions are better or worse rather than right or wrong ✔
  2. B. They have one correct answer
  3. C. They are solved by applying a standard formula
  4. D. They have clear goals and constraints

Answer: A

Examples: climate, poverty, healthcare.

Q30 Theory-based

Why does the course say Design Thinking exists?

  1. A. Well-defined problems cannot be solved by engineers
  2. B. Engineering education is useless for all kinds of problems
  3. C. Ill-defined and wicked problems do not yield to the methods that work on well-defined ones ✔
  4. D. Because linear pipelines are always faster

Answer: C

Traditional engineering education trains you superbly on well-defined problems; the other two kinds need iterative, human-centred methods.

Q31 Theory-based

Which statement is correct?

  1. A. Engineering training is best suited to wicked problems
  2. B. Ill-defined problems have clear goals and constraints
  3. C. Engineering training suits well-defined problems; ill-defined and wicked problems do not yield to the same methods, hence Design Thinking ✔
  4. D. Wicked problems have one correct answer; well-defined ones are better or worse

Answer: C

Well-defined: one correct answer. Ill-defined: many solutions. Wicked: complex, interconnected, better or worse.

Design Thinking: History & Frameworks

Q32 Case-based

Case: Two groups are given the same unfamiliar layout puzzle. Group X spends most of its time analysing the rules before attempting any arrangement. Group Y quickly tries many arrangements, learns from what fails, and refines.

In line with Lawson's experiment, which description is correct?

  1. A. X behaves like scientists (problem-focused); Y behaves like designers (solution-focused) ✔
  2. B. X behaves like designers (solution-focused); Y behaves like scientists (problem-focused)
  3. C. Both are solution-focused because both aim to finish the puzzle
  4. D. X is solution-focused and Y is problem-focused because Y analysed its failures

Answer: A

Lawson's key insight: scientists analyse thoroughly before proposing solutions; designers create and test many solutions rapidly, learning through iteration.

Q33 Case-based

Case: A start-up's smart lunchbox is loved by test users, and engineers confirm it can be manufactured. But the cost of making and shipping each unit is higher than the selling price, with no route to profit.

Which IDEO lens is failing, and what does that imply?

  1. A. Feasibility; the product cannot be built
  2. B. Desirability; users do not want the product
  3. C. Viability; meeting only two of the three lenses produces a concept that fails in practice ✔
  4. D. None; strong desirability alone guarantees innovation

Answer: C

IDEO's three overlapping lenses: desirability (do people want it?), feasibility (can we build it?), viability (is it economically sustainable?). Genuine innovation lies where all three overlap.

Q34 Case-based

Case: A company builds an internal HR portal using Plan → Build → Test → Ship over fourteen months. In launch week, employees say the navigation does not match how they work, and fixing it requires redoing large parts of the build.

Which characteristic of this process most directly caused the costly rework?

  1. A. User feedback arrives only at the end of a linear sequence, so changes are costly ✔
  2. B. The team iterated with users too often
  3. C. The team spent too long on empathy and prototyping
  4. D. The team followed the Stanford d.school loop too strictly

Answer: A

Traditional waterfall is linear, each phase finished before the next, with user feedback at the end. Human-centred design is iterative, with empathy, feedback, prototyping and refinement throughout (CO1).

Q35 Case-based

Case: Residents of a hostel complain about laundry. A team makes a cardboard-and-paper mock-up of a token-based drop-off counter and asks residents to pretend to use it while the team notes where they get confused.

  2. B. Define and Ideate
  3. C. Ideate and Prototype

Building a simple model is Prototype; evaluating it with users is Test. The results then feed back into earlier stages: the process is iterative, not linear.

Q65 Case-based

Never mix the two phases. When generating, defer all judgment; evaluate only after ideation is complete.

Divergent and convergent thinking are alternating phases, not rivals. Trouble starts when you converge too early (killing good ideas) or diverge too long (never deciding).

Narrowing options, evaluating and selecting = convergent.
