Unit 1 — Foundations of Learning, Creativity and Design Thinking

Q1.

A student spends two hours in deep focus solving a tricky circuit problem, gets nowhere, then goes for a walk and the answer suddenly appears. Which concept from Unit 1 best explains this?

  • A. The candle problem
  • B. Alternating focused and diffuse modes ✓
  • C. The VARK framework
  • D. Functional fixedness

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: The notes describe the practical rule that effective problem-solving alternates focused and diffuse modes — struggle first (focused), then step away so the diffuse mode can reorganise the material, as in the Mendeleev example.

Q2.

Mendeleev is said to have arranged the periodic table after dreaming about it. According to the Unit 1 notes, what is the key lesson from this story?

  • A. Dreams are a reliable source of scientific discovery
  • B. Diffuse mode does not work on an empty mind — struggle first, then step away ✓
  • C. Focused mode is always superior to diffuse mode
  • D. Creativity requires sleep deprivation

Correct Answer: B

Explanation: The notes explicitly state the diffuse mode reorganises material you have already struggled with in focused mode; it doesn't work on an empty mind.

Q3.

  • C. Visual learners actually perform worse than others

Explanation: The notes say the stronger claim — that being taught in your preferred style improves results — has repeatedly failed to hold up in controlled studies, even though VARK remains a useful self-reflection prompt.

Q6.

Q7.

In the plastic bucket worked example, students who list six or seven uses and then stall are advised to change their approach. What technique unlocks more ideas?

Q8.

Explanation: The expertise trap is over-reliance on past solutions — the more you know, the more you reach for what worked before.

Q21.

A concept is technically feasible and economically viable, but nobody actually wants it. Using the IDEO Human-Centred Design lens, which condition is missing, and what is the likely outcome?

Explanation: The IDEO framework requires all three lenses (Desirability, Feasibility, Viability) to overlap; meeting only one or two produces a concept that fails in practice — here, desirability (do people want this?) is missing.

Q22.

Explanation: This is the Waterfall model — linear, step-by-step, with each phase finished before the next; the notes state feedback comes at the end, so changes are costly, contrasted with HCD's iterative loop.
